The Challenge: A High-Authority Platform Invisible at the Moment of Highest Intent

Nexus Correspondent Bank & B2B Fintech serves the most demanding audience on the commercial web: B2B banking executives, CFOs, Chief Risk Officers, and credit union decision-makers who conduct vendor due diligence with high-density, authoritative answers — not generic consumer finance fluff. Yet the platform's highest-value commercial hubs — Relationship Profitability & Loan Pricing, CECL compliance, and Treasury Management — were structurally working against themselves.

A single commercial topic was dispersed across four primary tabs and two contradictory technical patterns: the Profitability hub over-fragmented into indexable sub-directory pages (Overview, Platform, Comparison) that competed for the same intent cluster, while the CECL hub buried its pricing and FAQ content behind client-side JavaScript hash silos that search engines discard entirely. A fourth path dumped high-intent buyers into a global tag archive with no conversion path — an unbounded crawl-budget sink.

The result: link-equity dilution across near-orphan pages, internal keyword cannibalization, and a body of content that modern AI answer engines could not assemble into a single, high-confidence source of truth. When a community-bank CFO asks an AI tool to shortlist profitability and CECL vendors, the citation goes to the competitor with a unified page.

The Strategic Cure: One Authoritative Asset per Vertical

1. Structural Re-Architecture of the Product Silos

I mapped every fragmented URL, tab layer, and hash anchor to a single canonical parent page per vertical — Loan Pricing positioned against precision competitors, CECL Compliance, and Treasury — via an 11-row consolidation matrix specifying one-hop 301s, on-page anchor sections, and server-rendering of every hash silo. The global tag-archive detour was severed from the conversion path entirely.

  • Canonical Promotion: /products/advisory-services elevated to the single authoritative long-form hub
  • Hash Silo Conversion: #overview / #platform / #pricing / #faq stripped and server-rendered into crawlable markup
  • Taxonomy Quarantine: /tags/ archive removed from the product journey; noindex,follow where thin

2. Nested Entity-Level Schema Graphs

To cement semantic trust with both the Knowledge Graph and LLM retrievers, I blueprinted a nested JSON-LD graph connecting FinancialService, LocalBusiness, and Person nodes — binding the advisory platform, the chartered institution, and its named subject-matter experts into one machine-readable trust chain, layered with WebPage, BreadcrumbList, and FAQPage markup.

3. Answer-Engine Readiness (GEO/AEO)

Each consolidated section was engineered as a self-contained, citable passage — opening with a declarative claim that co-locates capability, governing standard, and customer segment — so RAG pipelines and modern AI engines (ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, Gemini) can chunk, embed, and quote it with confidence. Deliverables Produced

The pilot was packaged as a C-suite-ready assessment — critique, execution matrix, architecture visualization, and phased implementation plan — designed so internal teams could act on it without a single follow-up meeting.

The Crawl Hygiene Sprint: A dev-ready Phase 1 package — one-hop 301 rules, server-rendering requirements for every hash silo, tag-taxonomy quarantine directives, and XML sitemap/GSC validation checkpoints across a 4–8 week consolidation window.

The Custom Internal-Linking Matrix: An 11-row URL-level execution table mapping every fragmented node to its canonical destination, anchor ID, and technical action — rebuilding mega-menu and in-content link equity flow around one authority URL per vertical.

The C-Suite Schema Blueprint: The nested FinancialService → LocalBusiness → Person entity graph, plus six drop-in copy remediations closing every identified ALCO retrieval gap — FTP methodology, named core integrations, RAROC regulatory context, peer benchmarking specs, quantified outcomes, and a regulatory alignment statement.

The 6-Month Content Roadmap: A five-phase implementation sequence — technical foundation, content consolidation, funnel realignment, monitoring & validation, and template rollout — extending the proven four-module pattern across the institution's remaining core verticals.
